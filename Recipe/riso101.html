riso 101.

risograph (ree-sow-graf) is best described as digital screenprinting. made in 1986 by riso kagaku corporation, the risograph is a high-speed, high-volume printer; it is sometimes described as a digital duplicator.

1. create or illustrate a design.

~for this recipe, please contain your design onto a 11x17" poster - max paper size for riso printing

2. separate the design into grayscale layers for each color - the darker it is, the more ink that the risograph will print

~the risograph thinks in grayscale - you can separate the colors manually like i do or put it onto spectrolite

3. save your files as a pdf and upload it onto google drive

4. reserve or walk-in at the design lab to use one of the riso printers

5. open your file and adjust printer settings accordingly (ink, master-making, paper size, etc)

~the risograph gives all designs a grain-touch appearance

~you can also choose to halftone your design, or have the risograph do it for you

6. clean the rollers to prevent track marks, adjust paper handling settings on printers

7. good luck printing! make sure to use uncoated paper, adjust speed settings if necessary!

~there will be misalignment, so you can choose to put reigstration marks

~the ink never fully dries, so the colors can mix together or start to bleed through the paper - this happens from 3 colors onwards
